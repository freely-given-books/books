# Proofread, 2026-10-10

The whole book (To the Reader and chapters I–IX, about 31,900 words) was
read for sense by two readers, each file compared with Goold's 1850 printing
(OCR of the archive.org scan), the 1890s American reprint and the 1658 first
edition. `sweep.py` and `refs.py` were run (154 references, none to a
missing verse). The corrections made before the proofread (about 50 CCEL
slips and dropped passages, and Owen's words where Goold departs from them)
are in `witness-report.md`.

## Questions

1. **ch. I, “when any duty is required that is extraordinary, that is a secret
   not often discovered. In the yoke of Christ it is a trial, a temptation.”**
   Goold's full stop after “discovered”. 1658: “that is a secret not often
   discovered, in the yoke of Christ; it is a triall, a temptation” (an
   extraordinary duty is a rare thing in Christ's yoke, and so a trial).
   *Recommend Owen's punctuation.*

## Left as is

- ch. III, “Populus sibilet, at mihi plaudo”: Horace has “Populus me
  sibilat”; Goold prints it so (1658 “nec mihi plaudo”), Owen quoting loosely.
- ch. III, “entrusted”: Goold “intrusted”; the edition keeps the modern spelling.
- ch. III, “out of temptations” (2 Pet. ii. 9, KJV).
- among (4) / amongst (5), farther (2) / further (1): all Goold's.
