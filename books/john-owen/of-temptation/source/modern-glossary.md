# Of Temptation: Modern English glossary

How the Modern English edition renders words that meant something else to Owen (false friends). The editor's rule (2026-11-16): such a word is replaced with a modern word in the text, chosen by context, never glossed; the sentence and Owen's voice stay. Scripture quotations (BSB as adjusted) and the Classic are untouched. The general rules are `MODERN-ENGLISH-RULES.md` at the repository root; the change list is `work/false-friends-2026-11-16/<book>.md`.

## False friends (2026-11-16)

| Owen | Modern English |
|---|---|
| professors (done before) | professing Christians |
| profession | profession of religion (where bare "profession" reads as a career; "the profession of piety" and Owen's image "this house of profession" stay); "our profession" as a body: the professing church |
| convinced persons | convicted persons |
| things indifferent | things neither good nor bad in themselves; good, evil, and neither good nor evil |
| surprised (by sin, by temptation) | overtaken (Gal 6:1) |
| passion | anger |
| preventing grace (our gloss removed) | grace that goes before us |
| active efficiency toward sinning (our gloss removed) | active force toward sinning |
| plantation | colony (Owen's own word a few sentences on) |
| own it, owning the lot | acknowledge, acknowledging |
| abroad | in private and in public; at large |
| want | lack |
| quick (sense, communion) | keen, lively |
| discoveries (of God's love) | revelations |
| lewdness (the world's) | vices |
| setting on (attacking) | attacking |
| visitation (outward) | affliction |
| the simple man … the strange woman | the naive man … the adulteress |
