# Of Temptation: wording decisions (1658 against Goold)

The editor's rule (2026-11-05): don't always prefer Goold; where the 1658 print and Goold's
text (1850) differ in wording, the reading text takes whichever works better in modern English,
with Goold as a good reference. Where they read equally well, the 1658 print stands, since
it is the base. Owen's errata (`errata.md`) win over both.

The differences were listed by script (`work/of-temptation/wording/wording.py`: the 1658 text
after the machine spelling pass against the reading text, word by word, leaving out
punctuation, case, references, numbering and spelling-close forms): 189 places.

## Kept from Goold (the rest; the list re-run after these changes has 151 places)

- **Spellings the machine pass leaves** (most of them): its self/itself, vv for w, possest, feavour, divell, oftner then, whore dome,
  quenchcoles, selfefullness, flyes/flees, where ever.
- **Owen's errata and the TCP's keying slips** (`errata.md`).
- **Grammar a modern reader needs**: agreement (if it makes), "he" for "be", "who" for
  "that" of persons, "will certainly" for "shall certainly", tenses in narrative (offered,
  besought, fell), "nor" in a series, a doubled "and", a stray "but" before a numbered head,
  "he considers all, what is acceptable, what is to be rejected".
- **Scripture where Goold gives the verse's sense**: Luke 22:46 "lest ye enter" (Owen's point
  is that Luke words it differently), "showing him all the kingdoms", "let him that thinketh
  he standeth", "integrity and uprightness", "whoredom and wine", "dwell upon the earth".
- **Clarity**: "says Peter", "To which I answer", "that is, to be taken by a temptation",
  "state, way, or condition", "in the foregoing chapters", "of such persons", "a toil",
  "entice", the order of the inquiries in chapter 4 (as the book goes on to treat them), Goold's
  Latin (Horace: "at mihi plaudo").
