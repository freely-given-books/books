# Calvin, Institutes, Volume 1 (prefaces + Book I): Modern English glossary (draft, 2026-11-23)

Binding for every drafter, reader and correction pass of the Modern English, Volume 1. Choices marked
**[editor]** shape the whole book and are put to the editor; the recommendation is applied meanwhile.

## The base text

- **The Modern is drafted from the "corrected Beveridge"** (`work/institutes/latin/vol1/corrected/`): Beveridge's
  1845 English with about 500 departures from Calvin's 1559 Latin put right (editor, 2026-11-22/23). The Classic
  (`chapters/typ/vol-1`) stays Beveridge as printed. Where the two differ, the Modern follows the corrected text.
  This is intended; it is not a Classic slip and is never undone.
- **Calvin's Latin of 1559 is the authority for sense** (`work/institutes/latin/vol1/la/`, and for Book I ch. 1-5
  `pilot/latin-1559-ch1-5.md`); his French of 1560 is the tiebreaker (`vol1/fr/`, `pilot/french-1560-ch1-5.md`).
  Where the English is unclear, the Latin decides.
- **Copyright.** No wording from Ford Lewis Battles (1960), McNeill, or any other in-copyright translation, even if
  remembered. Where a Modern sentence comes out the same as a remembered modern translation, reword it from the
  corrected Beveridge and the Latin. Public-domain wording (Beveridge 1845, Allen 1813/1841, Norton 1561) is free.
  Known Battles turns to avoid: "awareness of divinity" (I.3.1; we keep Beveridge's "sense of Deity"), "Nearly all
  the wisdom we possess, that is to say, true and sound wisdom" (I.1.1).

## Beveridge's own material

- **"Sections." synopses and argument paragraphs** at chapter heads are Beveridge's, not Calvin's. They stay,
  modernized like the text, labeled as Beveridge's (editor's decision):
  - the line `#block(inset: (x: 1em))[#emph[Sections.]]` → `#block(inset: (x: 1em))[#emph[Sections (Beveridge’s summary).]]`;
  - an argument paragraph before it (ch. 8, 11, 13, 14, 16, 18: "This chapter consists of …") opens with
    `#emph[Beveridge’s summary.]` inside its block: `#block(inset: (x: 1em))[#emph[Beveridge’s summary.] This chapter has two parts. …]`.
  - **[editor]** Book I's "Argument" (`book-1-argument.typ`) has no Latin behind it either; it is modernized, and
    left unlabeled until the editor says (recommendation: label it "Beveridge’s summary" too, under the heading).
- **Beveridge's notes** (`#footnote[… (Beveridge’s note.)]`): kept as notes, modernized lightly ("See Calvin on
  John 4:10."), the marker "(Beveridge’s note.)" kept exactly. Notes of bare references (Calvin's margin) stay as
  they are.

## Scripture (editor, 2026-11-23)

1. **Calvin's own form.** The corrected text restores Calvin's own wording of verses he paraphrases, argues from,
   or quotes in his own Latin form (Beveridge had often swapped in the KJV). Where the corrected text's
   quotation **differs from the KJV-style wording of the Classic** at that place, it is Calvin's form: keep it,
   modernized (has, you, today's words), **not** the BSB. Check the Latin where unsure.
2. **A plain quotation** (the corrected text = the Classic = KJV wording, and Calvin's Latin is simply the verse):
   BSB, adjusted lightly where Calvin leans on a word (rules §5, §7.2, §7.9).
3. Quote only what he quotes; a paraphrase stays his words.
4. Book I's leaned-on words to watch: "invisible things … clearly seen" (Rom 1:20, Calvin's "invisible things",
   not only "qualities"); "the worlds were framed" (Heb 11:3, the mirror of invisible things); "in him we live and
   move and have our being" (Acts 17:28); "God is Spirit" (John 4:24); "the heavens declare" (Ps 19:1); the
   attributes of Exod 34:6-7 and Jer 9:24 (I.10.2: Calvin expounds each; keep **mercy**, judgment, righteousness,
   truth where the Latin has misericordia / clementia, iudicium, iustitia, veritas, not the BSB's "loving
   devotion"); "Let us make man in our image, after our likeness" (Gen 1:26, I.15.3: image / likeness); the
   sparrows (Matt 10:29, "without your Father" / "apart from the will of your Father": Calvin's Latin "sine
   voluntate Patris"); "I form the light and create darkness; I make peace and create evil" (Isa 45:7, "evil" =
   adversity: Calvin's own explanation kept); "Shall there be evil in a city, and the Lord has not done it?"
   (Amos 3:6); the lying spirit (1 Kgs 22:20-22); "God sends them strong delusion" (2 Thess 2:11).
5. LORD → `#smallcaps[Lord]`; God's pronouns capitalized in BSB quotations as the BSB; in Calvin's prose as the base
   prints them.
6. Psalm numbers and references as the base prints them (the edition's style, Ps 19:1).

## Persons and address

- **you / you⁺**: every plural you, your, yours, yourselves marked `you#super[+]` (rules §4.2). The letter to King
  Francis is to one man: singular "you" (and "Your Majesty") unmarked; where Calvin turns to the opponents or the
  readers as many, marked. Scripture's "ye" marked.
- **Gendered language kept** (man, men, he, brethren, "no man").
- **Place names** (editor, 2026-11-22): Calvin's or Beveridge's old form kept, today's name in `\[brackets\]` after:
  Basle \[Basel\], Strasburg \[Strasbourg\], Ratisbon \[Regensburg\], Frankfort \[Frankfurt\], Spires \[Speyer\]
  (first time in each file). Ancient and biblical places stay as printed.

## Spelling and style

American spelling (honor, Savior, labor, center, theater, defense). Curly quotes. Gendered language kept.
"God" always capitalized; "Divine" lowercase as an adjective ("the divine essence") unless a title.

## Scripture settled

- Gal 4:8 "served those who by nature are not gods" (ch. 4 and 12; Calvin's δουλεία argument).
- John 8:44 "speaks from what is his own" (Latin ex propriis; ch. 14).
- 1 Thess 5:20 "Do not despise prophesying" (ch. 9).
- Gen 30:2 "who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?" (ch. 16).
- Isa 25:9 "Behold, this is our God … This, this is our God" (Calvin's two Latin forms, ch. 13).
- Num 23:19 "that He should change His mind" (Latin ut mutetur); 1 Sam 15:29 "repent" (ch. 17).
- Job 1:21 in Calvin's own (Vulgate) form "as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done" (ch. 18).
- The divine name is printed "Yahweh" in the Modern English (the editor, 2026-11-24, card #167): our added glosses are in square brackets (\[Yahweh\], rules §8.6), and Beveridge's own "(Jehovah)" and every "Jehovah" in his prose become "Yahweh" too. The Classic keeps "Jehovah" as printed.
- Dryden's Virgil (ch. 5) keeps its printed wording (rules §1.5).

## Footnotes (editor, 2026-11-23)

Every footnote that is Beveridge's own ends "(Beveridge’s note.)"; Calvin's own references (his 1559 text or margin)
are unmarked, decided note by note against the 1559 Geneva print (`work/institutes/latin/scan-1559-print`), not only
the 1869 Latin, whose Apparatus holds many of Calvin's margin references. Notes that mix Calvin's margin reference with
Beveridge's additions are left unmarked for the editor. The Classic stays unmarked, as printed.
