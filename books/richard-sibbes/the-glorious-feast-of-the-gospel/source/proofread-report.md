# The Glorious Feast of the Gospel: proofreading report

I read all of `chapters/typ` for sense: To the Reader and the nine sermons,
about 53,000 words. Each suspect place was checked against the 1650 printing
(the TEI's printed layer, with spelling modernized and nothing else
changed). The second witness is Grosart's 1862 text with his footnotes,
which marked doubtful places "Qu.". I used the Digital Puritan PDF of it,
from the user's Nextcloud folder; it is read only and is not in the repo.
The Monergism PDF named in the README is not on this machine. Earlier
choices recorded in `witness-report.md` were kept, such as "other men of
good wit", "always abounding" and "Christ said to the women".

## Needs your decision

Answer by number ("1 yes, 2 no, 3 as recommended"). Nothing here has been
applied.

1. **"etc." after a quoted verse.** The 1650 print has "&c." after six
   quotations, and the earlier copy dropped them all: sermon 1 twice ("drink
   wine with a song", "a feast of fat things"), sermon 3 ("cast over all
   people"), sermon 6 ("wine refined on the lees"), sermon 8 ("We have
   waited") and sermon 9 ("Lo, this is our God"). I tidied the stray ",'."
   this left behind. *Recommendation: leave them out.* The quotations read
   as complete.
2. **Sermon 3, "where it is taken off there is none of it."** In full: "And
   where this veil of ignorance is taken off, there is feasting with God … and
   where it is taken off there is none of it." The 1650 print and Grosart read
   the same. Grosart's footnote suggests "is *not* taken off", and the sense
   needs it. *Recommendation: yes, add "not".*
3. **Sermon 3, "God doth take away his veil."** The 1650 print reads "his".
   Grosart suggests "this veil", meaning the veil of ignorance just named.
   *Recommendation: yes, "this veil".*
4. **Sermon 3, "a spiritual eye, to see the things that to nature are
   visible."** The 1650 print reads the same. Grosart suggests "invisible",
   and the sentence before says "Divine things are invisible to natural
   eyes". *Recommendation: yes, "invisible".*
5. **Sermon 4, "there is not only light and knowledge, but removal of it
   ever may damp the feast."** The 1650 print and Grosart read the same, and
   it does not parse. The ninth sermon restates it as "removal of all that may
   hinder joy". *Recommendation: "but removal of whatever may damp the
   feast".*
6. **Sermon 3, "intritively"** (three times; the 1650 print and Grosart read
   the same). It is not an English word. Grosart guesses "intuitively", and the
   sense ("he seeth not intritively into the things themselves") agrees.
   *Recommendation: "intuitively".*
7. **Spellings that read wrongly today.** The 1650 print and the current text
   agree in each case.
   - Sermon 3: "to walk still to the high price of God's calling". Phil. 3:14
     reads "prize". *Recommendation: "prize".*
   - Sermon 4: "full of troubles and combers". Grosart glosses it "cumbers".
     *Recommendation: "cumbers".*
   - Sermon 1: "straight receivers of the sacrament", meaning narrow.
     *Recommendation: "strait".*
   - Sermon 1 has "stricken" (with the rod of Moses) and sermon 7 has
     "strucken". *Recommendation: "stricken" in both.*
8. **One form throughout the book.** Each item gives the counts and my
   recommendation:
   - Book names in references: Mat ×24 / Matthew ×1, Philip ×12 /
     Philippians ×2, Ephes ×1 / Ephesians ×2, 2 Chron ×1. Every other book
     is written out. *Recommendation: write them all out (Matthew,
     Philippians, Ephesians, 2 Chronicles).*
   - Song of Songs ×5 (To the Reader) / Song of Solomon ×2 (sermon 9).
     *Recommendation: Song of Solomon (KJV).*
   - St ×5 / Saint ×2 (sermon 3 "Saint Paul's study", sermon 9 "the apostle
     Saint James"). *Recommendation: St.*
   - "any thing" ×1 and "every thing" ×3 are left over; the edition elsewhere
     has anything and everything. *Recommendation: anything, everything.*
   - "noon-day" (To the Reader) / "noonday" (sermon 6). *Recommendation:
     noonday.*
   - "where-ever" (sermon 8). *Recommendation: wherever.*
   - "Goliah" (To the Reader, the 1650 form). *Recommendation: Goliath
     (KJV), as the book already uses Samson and Zion.*
9. **References the earlier editor added that point at the wrong verse.**
   The 1650 print has no reference at these places, or a bare chapter. I
   fixed only those that were plainly mistyped (below).
   - To the Reader, "as David 'the head of Goliah,' 1 Samuel 31:9" (that
     verse is Saul's head) → **1 Samuel 17:54**.
   - To the Reader, "'the spirit of glory rests upon them,' 2 Corinthians
     12:9" → **1 Peter 4:14**.
   - Sermon 2, Barzillai, "2 Samuel 19:32" → **19:35**.
   - Sermon 3, "'darkness itself,' 2 Corinthians 6:14" → **Ephesians 5:8**.
   - Sermon 4, "Colossians 2:10, upon the cross Christ did nail the law …
     principalities and powers" (the 1650 print has "Coll. 2.") →
     **Colossians 2:14, 15**.
   - Sermon 4, "If by his death we are saved, much more …", "Romans 5:21" →
     **Romans 5:10**.
   - Sermon 4, "'We sit in heavenly places already with Christ,' Ephesians
     1:8" → **Ephesians 2:6**.
   - Sermon 4, "'the law of the spirit of life … hath freed me from the law of
     sin and death,' Romans 6:7" → **Romans 8:2**.
   - Sermon 6, "Moses's speech … 'They shall be a hissing to all nations,' 2
     Chron 29:8". Moses' words are in Deuteronomy 28:37 ("a proverb, and a
     byword, among all nations"). *Recommendation: Deuteronomy 28:37.*
   - Sermon 9, "as the angel saith, 'it is done,' Revelation 19:17" (Grosart
     has the same) → **Revelation 16:17**.

   *Recommendation: yes to all.*
10. **Sermon 9, "as when the children of Israel came from Babylon, they had
    wells by the way, as in Micah, they digged up wells."** The 1650 print
    reads "as in Michae". The earlier copy guessed "Micah", but Micah says
    nothing about wells. Grosart suggests the valley of Baca, Psalm 84:6
    ("Who passing through the valley of Baca make it a well").
    *Recommendation: "as in the valley of Baca, Psalm 84:6, they digged up
    wells".* Alternatively keep "Micah" and accept the doubt.
11. **"has" for "hath" in two places.** These are sermon 4, "It has become a
    drone" (1650 "Its become"), and sermon 6, "Thus it has been" (1650
    "hath"). Both come from Grosart, and the rest of the book keeps "hath".
    *Recommendation: "It is become a drone" and "Thus it hath been", as in
    1650.*

### Words with their tense or form changed

- Sermons 5 and 9: "when he saw that men *break* the law" and "they *break*
  out in this manner" are now "brake", as in 1650 and Grosart. The copy had
  made them present tense.
- Sermon 2 "over much" and sermon 4 "fear over much" are now "overmuch".
  This is the 1650 form there and the book's form everywhere else.
- Sermon 5: "Why should I smite them *anymore*?" is now "any more"
  (Isa. 1:5).
- Sermon 3: "They shall know that do *practice*" is now "practise". The book
  uses practise for the verb.
- Sermon 8: "for *to morrow* we shall die" is now "tomorrow", as in the next
  line.

### Quotations

- Sermon 1: "'not to be high-minded, but fear; for, saith he, 'if God …" was
  never closed. It is now "but fear;' for, saith he, 'if God …".
- Sermon 4: "'If it be possible, let this cup pass from me that was nature"
  is now "from me;' that was nature".
- Sermon 9: "things to come. 'Why?' Because you are Christ’s,'" is now
  "things to come.' Why? 'Because you are Christ’s,' 1 Corinthians 3:22, 23".
- Sermon 8: "'for to him a thousand years are but as one day,'" ended the
  paragraph on a comma. It now ends "one day.'".
- Places where "&c." was dropped and left ",'." or ",';" are now ".'" or
  "';". These are "with a song", "fat things" and "We have waited", plus "Lo,
  this is our God';".

### Punctuation and spacing

- Sermon 1: "everlasting glory 'In this mountain" now has a full stop, as in
  1650. "What thing is this?. So" is now "this? So".
- Sermon 5: "the cause of grief And as" and "hereafter, If no tears" are now
  full stops.
- Sermon 4: "callings, employments but then" now has a comma, as in 1650.
- Sermon 7: "the church ever had The ten persecutions" now has a full stop,
  and "apprehension of The state" is now lowercase.
- Sermon 9: "my times are in thy hands that is" now has a comma, and "to be
  with, him" has lost its stray comma.
- Glued and spaced words: "So-there" is now "So there", "and-body" is now
  "and body", "holy-feast" is now "holy feast" (1650 "holy Feast"), and
  "gaudy - day" is now "gaudy-day".
- To the Reader: "'all things ready,' Mat 22:4  for" is now "Mat 22:4, for".
  Double spaces in "come  and freely" and "they are  θεόπνευστοι" are
  removed.
- Sermon 6: "Cesar’s" is now "Caesar’s", as in 1650 and in sermon 6's
  "Caesar" a page earlier.

## Checked and kept

- Period English is kept: hath, -eth, ye, "these that be his", "most
  excellentest", "for to take away", "jailor", "camphire", "poisonful",
  "Catholic Protestantial church", "aqua vitae", "sapida scientia" and
  "Aye, but".
- Readings that look odd but are the 1650 text: "raise up mists from our own
  mists" (sermon 5), "He can find tears for other things, matter of this
  enough" (sermon 6), "Our time shall be hereafter at the day of the
  resurrection" (sermon 3), "Mens, Lux" (sermon 3), "thirty-four years"
  (sermon 8), "whether he will hang with the greatest worldling" (sermon 9),
  "to fetch sorrow from the devil" (sermon 8), "rugged winds" (sermon 5) and
  "Augustine" once among five "Austin"s (sermon 4; the 1650 print has both).
- Sermon 3, "a light radicated in the soul". Grosart notes that something
  may be lost here, but 1650 reads the same. I kept it, because a fix would
  be a guess.
- Sermon 6, "that he is a jewel to God in his esteem, to be absolutely the
  best thing in the world." The sentence is unfinished in 1650 too.
- `refs.py` lists 19 weak quote matches, all read and kept. They are loose
  paraphrases (Psalm 41:3, Philip 4:4, 2 Corinthians 5:13-14) or false hits
  ("Psalm 115:3" introduces the quotation after it). The wrong verses among
  them are question 9.
- The paragraph breaks before "Wouldst thou have peace" and "Then, indeed,
  manna" in sermon 1 are in the 1650 print, so they stay.
