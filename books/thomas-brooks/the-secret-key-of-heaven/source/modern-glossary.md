# The Secret Key of Heaven: Modern English glossary

How the Modern English edition renders Brooks's recurring terms, labels and verses (set 2026-11-16, when the
Modern English was drafted by thirteen hands). Brooks's *Precious Remedies Against Satan's Devices* (1653) was
done first; its glossary (`../precious-remedies-against-satans-devices/source/modern-glossary.md`) is carried
over here so the same author's recurring words render the same way. A change here is the editor's to make, and
is then applied to the whole book. The general rules are `MODERN-ENGLISH-RULES.md` at the repository root.

## Closet (the book's own term; put to the editor)

Brooks's subject is **closet prayer** ("closet" 454 times: closet prayer, closet duties, closet communion, closet
experiences, "keep close to your closet"). Rules §3.6 lists *closet* (private room) as a false friend, but here it
is the author's own technical term, the word of his text (Matt 6:6, ταμεῖον "rendered *closet*", which he defines
in the Introduction as "a secret chamber or close and locked parlor"), the subtitle's word, and still current
Christian English ("prayer closet"). So **"closet" stays, everywhere, as the book's term**; "chamber" → "room"
where it is a plain room. "Private prayer" and "secret prayer" (his synonyms) stay as he varies them. Option for
the editor: "private room" / "private prayer" throughout (a scripted change by sense).

## The text: Matt 6:6, and the verses Brooks builds on

BSB adjusted lightly where Brooks leans on the KJV's word:

- **Matt 6:6** “But when you pray, go into your closet, shut your door, and pray to your Father, who is in
  secret. And your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you openly.” (BSB "inner room" → *closet*, the word he
  expounds; "who is unseen" / "sees what is done in secret" → *in secret*, his whole argument; *openly*, the
  received text, Argument 5 "Secret Duties Shall Have Open Rewards". The "you" is singular, unmarked: Brooks argues
  from the singular "thou".) The block over the Introduction keeps its layout, italic, no quotation marks.
- **Matt 6:4, 6:18** “… and your Father, who sees in secret, will reward you openly.”
- **Rom 8:15** “… you#super[+] received the Spirit of adoption, by whom we cry, ‘Abba! Father!’” (*adoption*, his heading)
- **Gal 4:6** “And because you#super[+] are sons, God sent the Spirit of His Son into your hearts, crying out, ‘Abba, Father!’”
  (*your hearts*, as Brooks quotes it)
- **Rom 8:26** “… the Spirit helps us in our infirmities …” (*infirmities*, Objection 6); “groans too deep for words” (BSB)
- **Zech 12:10** “a spirit of grace and supplication” (the heading "a Spirit of Prayer and Supplication")
- **Jas 5:16** “The effectual, fervent prayer of a righteous man has great power.” (*effectual, fervent*: he expounds them)
- **Ps 25:14** “The secret of the #smallcaps[Lord] is with those who fear Him, and He reveals His covenant to them.” (*secret*, Argument 12)
- **Ps 83:3** “… they conspire against Your hidden ones.” (*hidden ones*, Argument 20, "or Thy secret ones")
- **Song 2:14** “O my dove, in the clefts of the rock, in the secret places of the stairs …” (BSB "crevices of the
  cliff" adjusted: Brooks leans on the word, "in the clefts of the rock, or under the stairs"; settled in the audit)
- **Ps 55:17** “Evening, morning, and noon I pray and cry aloud, and He hears my voice.” (*pray*: his proof of set times)
- **Ps 5:3** “… in the morning I will direct my prayer to You and will look up.” (he expounds both words)
- **Mark 1:35** “Early in the morning, a great while before day, Jesus got up …” (he repeats "a great while before day")
- **Eph 6:18** “Pray in the Spirit always, with every kind of prayer and supplication …”
- **Ezek 36:37** “For this, too, I will be sought by the house of Israel, to do it for them.”
- **Jer 3:3** “Yet you have a harlot’s forehead …” (his prose echoes it: "a harlot's forehead")
- **Hos 12:4** “Yes, he had power over the angel and prevailed; he wept and made supplication to Him.”
- **Gen 32:24-30** BSB, with "wrestled" and "prevailed" kept wherever Brooks leans on them; v. 28 where he quotes
  “As a prince hast thou power with God”: “as a prince you have power with God”.
- **1 Thess 5:17** “Pray without ceasing.” (BSB)

## Labels

`Obj.` / `Object.` → `Objection.`; `Ans.` / `Answ.` → `Answer.`; `Quest.` → `Question.`; `Doctrine:` stays.
`etc.` stays (roman).

## Quotations and italics

The Classic already sets Scripture in quotation marks with the reference in parentheses; that stays, the words
from the BSB as adjusted above, "…" where Brooks trims a verse. Brooks's emphases keep their italics; a word of a
verse he picks out to expound keeps its italics. Untranslated Latin keeps its words with a [bracketed
translation]; where Brooks translates it himself, nothing is added. Greek and Hebrew stay as printed.

## Plural you

`you#super[+]` (your⁺, You⁺) marks a plural "you": Scripture's "ye"/"you" to many; Brooks to his readers in
the plural ("yourselves", "friends", "Christians", "you that …"). The first plural you/your of EVERY sentence of a
plural address is marked, the rest of that sentence plain. Brooks's "thou" to the single reader is plain "you".
