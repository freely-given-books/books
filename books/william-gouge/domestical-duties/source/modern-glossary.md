# Of Domestical Duties: Modern English glossary

How the Modern English edition renders Gouge's recurring terms, labels and verses. Set by Volume I
(2026-11-15); **Volumes II-IV follow it**. A change here is the editor's to make, and is then applied
to every volume already drafted. The general rules are `MODERN-ENGLISH-RULES.md` at the repository root.

## Layout kept exactly

- Chapter titles (`==`) are the edition's own, already modern. Gouge's section heads
  (`=== §. N. #emph[Of …]`) keep their level, number, italics and any margin footnote, but their wording is
  modernized like the body (the editor, 2026-11-15, on "betwixt"): between, a husband, wife's, has, -s
  verbs; church lowercase and baptism, justification, saints, papists, pope as the body has them;
  Saviour → Savior. A partly italic head (`#emph[Of the resemblance between] the church's …`) keeps its
  split.
- The text blocks over a section (`#align(center)[#block …]`) keep their layout; only the verse words
  inside change, set in italic without quotation marks.
- Paragraphs match the Classic one for one; the 1622 breaks before an italic doctrine stay
  ("… teaches us that" / new paragraph).

## Italics

Doctrines and emphases keep their italics; Scripture quotations are roman in curly quotation marks;
italic names become roman; a word of a verse Gouge picks out to expound keeps its italics.

## The text of the treatise (Eph 5:21–6:9)

BSB, adjusted where Gouge expounds the King James wording:

- Eph 5:21 Submit to one another in the fear of God.
- 5:22 Wives, submit to your⁺ own husbands as to the Lord.
- 5:23 For the husband is the head of the wife, as Christ is the head of the church; and He is the Savior of the body.
- 5:24 Therefore, as the church is subject to Christ, so let wives be subject to their own husbands in everything.
- 5:25 Husbands, love your⁺ wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave Himself up for her.
- 5:26 that He might sanctify her and cleanse her with the washing of water by the word.
- 5:27 that He might present her to Himself a glorious church, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing, but that she should be holy and without blemish.
- 5:28 So men ought to love their wives as their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself.
- 5:29 For no man ever yet hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, just as the Lord does the church.
- 5:30 For we are members of His body, of His flesh, and of His bones.
- 5:31 For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will be one flesh.
- 5:32 This is a great mystery, but I am speaking about Christ and the church.
- 5:33 Nevertheless, let each one of you⁺ in particular so love his wife as himself, and let the wife see that she reverences her husband.
- 6:1 Children, obey your⁺ parents in the Lord, for this is right.
- 6:2 “Honor your father and mother” (which is the first commandment with a promise),
- 6:3 that it may go well with you, and that you may live long on the earth.
- 6:4 And you⁺ fathers, do not provoke your children to wrath, but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.
- 6:5-9 (Volume IV): to be set the same way when drafted, from the BSB, keeping the words Gouge expounds.

Other recurring verses: 1 Pet 3:7 "the weaker vessel" (kept); Gen 2:24 and Matt 19:5-6 as the BSB
("one flesh"; "what God has joined together, let man not separate").

## Words

Kept (Gouge's terms, the Reformed register): calling (general / particular), vocation, family (it
includes servants; "household" where the sense is the whole house), household government, superiors /
inferiors, subjection, submission, reverence, the fear of God, doctrine, use (a sermon's head),
mystery, sanctify / sanctification, justification, glorification, the elect, the saints, effectual,
the flesh, the spirit (the regenerate part), means, ordinance, sacrament, popish / papists, head /
headship, spouse, due benevolence (glossed once a chapter: "the marriage duty each owes the other").

- church: lowercase (as the BSB), but "the Church of Rome"; "the Catholic Church" → "the catholic
  church", with "(the universal church)" at its first use in a chapter.
- Holy Ghost → Holy Spirit; Saviour → Savior.
- God always capitalized; Gouge's own pronouns for God as the Classic prints them; the BSB's in its quotations.
- Gendered language kept (man, men, he, brethren, no man).
- you⁺: the first plural you/your of every sentence of a plural address.

False friends (old sense → plain word): honest (honorable, decent, chaste), discover (reveal,
expose), let (hinder), meat (food), nice (fussy), presently (at once), prevent (go before),
conversation (conduct), entertain (receive), indifferent (neither commanded nor forbidden),
convenient (fitting), competent (sufficient), comfortable (strengthening), estate (condition), mean
(lowly), several (separate), peculiar (one's own), still (always), wit (understanding), silly
(simple), naughty (wicked), affection (desire, feeling), careful (anxious), quick (living), fond
(foolish), froward (perverse), cousin (kinsman).

## Added from the Volume I drafts and audit

The text block of Eph 5:22 reads "Wives, submit yourselves⁺ to your own husbands as to the Lord." (§12 argues
from the reflexive, middle voice "submit your selves"); 5:21 is quoted "Submitting to one another …" where
Gouge argues from the participle (§1); 5:26 "having cleansed her" where he argues from the order (§39).

Recurring Scripture words kept where Gouge leans on them (the BSB adjusted): "fear" (Rom 13:7, Col 3:22,
Isa 29:13), "our goodness does not extend to" God (Ps 16:2), "be subject to your husband" (Gen 3:16), "seek
its own" (1 Cor 13:5, Phil 2:21), "without natural affection" (Rom 1:31, 2 Tim 3:3), "no spot" (Song 4:7),
"impute" (Ps 32:2), "a new creature" (2 Cor 5:17), "his own flesh" (Isa 58:7), "who makes you different"
(1 Cor 4:7), "resurrection of condemnation" (John 5:29), "by one Spirit" (1 Cor 12:13), "dwell in Him"
(1 John 4:13), "Make no provision for the flesh, to gratify its lusts" (Rom 13:14), "let us be content"
(1 Tim 6:8), "they will prolong your days" (Exod 20:12), "appointed time" (Job 7:1, 14:14), "falls away
from the grace of God" (Heb 12:15), "Train up a child in the trade of his way" (Prov 22:6, Gouge's play on
"trade").
